# Work or Proof? · Open questions

Append-only dated entries. These are questions and candidate extensions, not approved scope or findings.

## 2026-11-11 (HKT) · Survey and draft protocol

- **Can a first check save anything if it almost never clears work?** The proposed violation-or-abstention gate escalates 1,372/1,440 primary items and every Rebel item. Is the useful design variable an evidence gate with measurable selectivity, rather than adding another monitor stage? A different gate needs a separate pre-specified comparison.
- **Should a review service optimize tokens or dollars?** Report-only uses fewer total tokens but more saved dollars in every main cohort. Could concise-answer constraints change the result without harming detection? The longer answers include accounted output beyond the short visible verdict; prompt brevity alone may not control it. This requires new calls and is outside v0.1.
- **Does a conversational second stage behave like a fresh trace judge?** Showing a monitor its earlier accusation or abstention could cause correction, anchoring or persistence. The saved stateless trace verdicts cannot answer this. Would an independent second-stage family help, at a measured cost?
- **What should happen after a trace-stage abstention?** Rebel has 80 clean trace abstentions and no breach trace abstentions. Keeping an earlier accusation, withholding a decision and asking a human have different operational costs; this protocol retains the trace abstention and reports it explicitly.
- **Can cheap clearances be trusted?** HA Claude report-only clears 68 items, including 26 breaches. Triage that escalates only positive/unknown verdicts never revisits those false clearances. Would a random audit floor protect against this blind spot more efficiently than the proposed gate?
- **How much of the apparent dollar difference is transport accounting?** GPT has roughly 14,000 accounted tokens per judgment; Claude aggregates cached and uncached inputs at one historical rate. Raw billing components are absent. What would a cache-aware direct-API cost comparison show, and could original non-secret usage exports resolve it without new inference?
- **Which instruction creates the accusation workload?** On the same HA work, the GPT breach monitor's report-only view flags 9 items, while the two-question referee's rules answer flags 246. Model family alone cannot explain the difference; the instruction and judgment task changed. Does a clearer compliance instruction improve detection enough to justify its false alarms and escalations?
- **How should the replay generalize beyond its archive mix?** Rebel contributes 75% of primary work, has much higher breach prevalence and only five shared seeds. Different breach prevalence, trace lengths or hidden-denial rates may change which policy pays. A later prospective study should vary those factors without relabeling this replay as unseen-data evidence.
- **Where does checking stop creating value?** The toy fixes production and makes verification perfect; this replay fixes production and makes verification empirical. Neither observes a joint production/checking budget, queue delays, human intervention cost or accepted useful work in deployment. What is the smallest later environment that measures that missing link?

## 2026-11-11 (HKT) · Agreement r1 response / v0.1 draft r2

The entries above preserve the original zero-call proposal. Protocol r2 now includes prospective local monitoring; its scope and fixed predictions supersede that proposal without rewriting the question history.

- **Can trace screening preserve catches while saving frontier tokens?** This is now the primary question, using Qwen 7B LT versus X; Llama, local-report screening and local-only checking are fixed secondary comparisons. Does escalation select useful evidence, or merely suppress difficult breaches through false clearance?
- **How costly is the supposedly free screen in practice?** Saved HA actor-report timings imply a 25.15-minute serial baseline projection for 5,808 local requests, and a 5.59-hour output-cap-scaled scenario. Neither measures monitor latency. What are the actual prompt/output lengths, failed-call costs and elapsed times? The protocol records them and imposes a cumulative 12-hour collection cap; energy/hardware costs remain unavailable.
- **Does seeded replay transfer from actors to monitors?** Qwen's historical actor replay and Llama's observed recheck variation motivate 24 predetermined replays/model. Will exact raw-text agreement differ from verdict agreement? First responses remain the analysis inputs even when replays disagree.
- **Does same-family screening create a blind spot?** Local actors and monitors share Qwen/Llama families. The fixed actor-by-monitor breakdown can describe this association, but a causal claim needs a later controlled study.
- **Would an independent frontier family change Rebel conclusions?** The optional paid GPT Rebel arm is omitted from v0.1, not left as a result-dependent option. A later separately agreed version would need a specific information gain and a measured current budget; the archived HA GPT cohorts remain limited secondary evidence.
