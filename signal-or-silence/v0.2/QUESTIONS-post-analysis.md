# Signal or Silence · post-analysis questions

Append-only. These questions were selected **post hoc** on 11 November 2026 after
the first frozen analysis. They are hypotheses for a separately agreed, frozen

## 2026-11-11 · Frozen v0.1 results, Codex

- Why did Claude abstain on 97.50% of truncated-history views but 0% of
  withheld-authorization views under the plain instruction? Does “unavailable”
  authorization get interpreted as absent authorization, while explicit omitted
  events trigger uncertainty? A future wording/marker manipulation could
  distinguish these possibilities without claiming that this study isolated them.
- Why did the explicit instruction raise Claude W abstention to 88.33% while
  leaving T near its existing ceiling, yet reduce Llama T abstention from 37.50%
  to 12.08% and raise decisive F/R false alarms to 90.00%/95.83%? Which instruction
  clauses or task interpretations matter? Those changes are consistent with
  instruction/presentation sensitivity, not proof of a shared internal mechanism.
- How much of a judge's usable reliability comes from semantic inference versus
  compliance with a machine-readable output contract? Claude had 275 scored
  technical failures across unique calls, including explanatory prose plus JSON
  and the retained D1 overrun flag. A new prospective comparison could enforce
  equivalent output constraints across transports. This does not license a
  permissive rescore or rescue of the present frozen outcomes.
- Which repeated evidence patterns drive trajectory versus equal-evidence gaps?
  For example, explicit Qwen R recall is 60.83% by trajectory and 83.33% by
  decisive-payload weighting; its R abstention is 44.17% versus 23.01%. A larger
  unseen-template benchmark could test whether the gap persists without treating
  repeated seeds or inputs as independent tasks.
- Would judges seek the particular missing receipt that resolves ambiguity in a
  sequential evidence-request task, rather than make a definite accusation or
  repeatedly ask for irrelevant evidence? Fixed-view abstention cannot establish
  useful evidence-seeking behavior.
- Does categorical logical possibility remain useful when histories have highly
  unequal prior probabilities or receipts are untrusted? The current witness test
  establishes possibility under a trusted simulator, not equal likelihood,
  probability calibration or real-world receipt authenticity.

## 2026-11-12 HKT · v0.2 frozen follow-up, Codex · post hoc

This continues the byte-preserved v0.1 log above. The root question log is an
inherited frozen input; this versioned continuation preserves its complete
prefix. Questions are hypotheses, not conclusions or permission for collection.
The current protocol ends scientific extension after v0.2; any future study
requires a separate agreement rather than a v0.3 or quality rerun here.

- Why does certified ambiguity trigger almost universal “can’t tell” on truncated
  history (99.17%) but none on withheld authorization receipts under the plain
  v0.2 instruction? On W, the judge accuses 82.35% of the 68 reference-compliant
  trajectories, while plain primary F/R false alarms are zero. Is the meaning
  assigned to an unavailable receipt different from an explicitly omitted event?
  These arms differ in composition and presentation, so this contrast does not
  isolate the cause.
- What makes the explicit sufficiency instruction move W abstention from 0% to
  97.50% while leaving T at 99.17%? Could a separately frozen, unseen-task study
  distinguish evidence-sensitive abstention from reliance on omission markers
  or instruction cues? This dataset alone establishes neither mechanism.
- Why does the identical added format sentence leave plain F/R validity at
  90.42%/86.67%, while explicit F/R reaches 95.83%/94.17%? Are strict-output
  failures associated with particular evidence groups, reply-envelope handling,
  or the interaction between instruction content and output control? A diagnostic
  could inspect saved failures without changing the registered scores.
- How should deployed reliability be judged when breach recall clears 90% and
  false alarms are zero, but more than one in ten plain complete-evidence replies
  fails the output contract? The v0.2 joint rule rejects that pattern. Would a
  separately tested downstream abstention/escalation policy preserve useful
  decisions without concealing invalid replies or weakening denominators?
- Why do repeated evidence patterns shift v0.2 plain F validity from 90.42% by
  trajectory to 86.46% by equal evidence, while R shifts from 86.67% to 90.77%?
  Which patterns concentrate output failures, and would that pattern persist on
  unseen templates? Repeated trajectories cannot answer generalization questions.
- How much semantic information is obscured by strict output failures? The post
  hoc v0.1 single-object extraction recovers 274 prose-wrapped replies and gives
  100% F/R recall, while retaining the D1 integrity flag. A new study would need
  an independently fixed extraction contract and unseen tasks; this diagnostic
  supplies no registered pass and cannot retroactively repair v0.1.
- Would a judge that says “can’t tell” request the one receipt that resolves the
  ambiguity? High abstention on fixed views does not establish useful evidence
  seeking, nor does zero plain W abstention establish deliberate overconfidence.
