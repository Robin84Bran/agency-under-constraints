# The Useful Core · Questions

## 2026-11-11 · Stage-2 review decisions

1. **Instruction effect or architecture effect?** v0.1 deliberately tests fallback-priority versus full-job-priority text with identical partial-work permission, toolchain and utilities. It cannot carry the toy's modular-architecture cost claim. Proposed decision: accept this narrow question and retain the distinction in results/page copy.
2. **What is useful?** Assigned values 12/6/2 reward rendered, checked and raw receipt-based bulletins. They are synthetic assumptions; no model judges artifact quality. Proposed decision: retain values from the toy, report every tier and oracle attainment, and make no human-value calibration claim.
3. **Does the joint pass rule answer the intended question?** H counts only accurately reported useful work, treating abstention as a miss, while A and false full claims remain separate. Improved H does not prove lower overclaiming. Proposed decision: accept the two utility endpoints and require the separate honesty table; do not describe an H win as an honesty-rate win.
4. **Will the completion report be too easy?** Accepted-delivery receipts explicitly reveal the committed tier. This gives the model strong evidence, intentionally isolating inaccurate reporting despite sufficient evidence. A ceiling is informative, but weak evidence about reporting under ambiguity. Proposed decision: do not hide receipts merely to force errors; leave missing receipts to Receipt Gap/Signal or Silence.
5. **Are two outage positions enough?** Early leaves only core and late leaves reduced. This provides a clear counterfactual ceiling with six decisions; no recovery/repair regime is included. Proposed decision: keep this bounded v0.1 and avoid broad outage-distribution claims.
6. **Is the frontier contrast affordable and informative?** Fix 60 episodes in ten complete seed blocks, after local collection, with no sample change based on local results. Exact sizing is still required at build stage. Proposed decision: use empirical reservations, USD25 envelope including USD2 protected reserve, actual settlement and USD39 project stop; do not resurrect an unprovable aggregate-output-cap gate.
7. **Relationship to One Door.** The older research brief suggested a merger, while the current loop/delegation treats Wave 3 projects separately. Proposed decision: keep this study about partial deliverables and One Door about capacity/failure/queues; revisit editorial grouping only after distinct evidence exists.

## 2026-11-11 · Follow-up hypotheses outside v0.1

- Does an explicit fallback priority improve planning, or only tell a compliant model when to stop chasing the full target? The two-text intervention estimates their combined effect. A later matched-length cue/no-utility-table factorial could separate mechanisms.
- Would utility rankings that vary between cases reveal whether models maximize value rather than follow the words full/reduced/core? Fixed rankings here cannot distinguish the two.
- If outages repair after an unknown delay, when does waiting beat immediate delivery of the core? This requires a separate stopping problem and new oracle; repeated retries here never restore a tool.
- Does committing a core deliverable early and then upgrading it outperform a single terminal delivery? v0.1 deliberately has one commitment, so it cannot answer this.
- Would scarce observation or missing health information make a feasibility registry valuable? Here both arms have the same reliable error feedback and no free health oracle.
- How do models behave when a smaller artifact is harmful without a warning? Here every tier is explicitly useful and permitted; partial delivery is not universally good.
- Does fallback prompting retain the same advantage on open-ended artifacts scored by deterministic tests, where a receipt alone cannot establish quality? A future engineering task could supply that stronger external-validity test.
- Can an agent state the correct limitation while failing to deliver the available artifact? Publish artifact-obtained versus committed counts to distinguish verbal caution from useful completion.
- If false full claims occur despite explicit delivery receipts, do they track confusion between tool success, attempted delivery and committed delivery? Saved trajectories can support descriptive categories; intent remains unmeasured.
- Are instructions shared across model families comparable after different chat templates/default systems? Exact identity checks prevent accidental substitutions but do not erase training or formatting differences.
- How often do real frontier costs exceed empirical reservations because of hidden requests or schema activity? Keep usage/settlement discrepancies visible. An empirical overrun is evidence to stop and revise, not a reason to rewrite the ledger or omit failed calls.
- Does the framing effect survive genuinely different task dependency graphs? Seeds here change identifiers, receipt strings and local sampling, while repeating three structural worlds. A large episode count must not be presented as broad task diversity.
